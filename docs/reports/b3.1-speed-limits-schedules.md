## 1. Speed limits (CORE-06): what works

- **Engine (`dm-backend-http`):**
  - one global token bucket and one per download, shared by that download's segments;
  - burst = max(rate / 4, 1 KiB), so at most about 250 ms of data;
  - writes are split into parts no larger than the burst and charged to the job bucket, then the global one;
  - live rate changes wake waiters (`watch`) without a new request;
  - waits end at once on pause, cancel or shutdown (dropped futures);
  - unlimited is one atomic load with no wait;
  - throttled segments rotate their connection after 1 MiB or 2 s when others wait.
- **Correction (a real defect found by test S05):**
  - the defect: reqwest's client `read_timeout` kept running while the engine waited for tokens, so slow limits
    failed with `NetworkOffline`;
  - the fix: the client no longer sets it, and the engine owns the per-read timeout (`transfer::within`) around
    sends and body reads only.
- **Contract (`service-api.v1`):**
  - methods `speed_limits.v1.get`, `speed_limits.v1.set` and `downloads.v1.set_speed_limit`;
  - the optional `speed_limit_bytes_per_second` on add and on snapshots;
  - values are decimal `ByteCount` strings from 1024 to 10^12, enforced by the schema pattern and by Rust;
  - `null` means unlimited (global) or "only the overall limit" (job); `0` is refused, because it is not pause.
- **Service:**
  - persist, then apply; a refused save changes neither the stored nor the effective limit (trigger test);
  - restored before recovery: the global rate reaches the backend first, and recovered attempts carry their own
    limit;
  - gated on `speed_limit_global` / `speed_limit_per_job` = `supported`, otherwise
    `BACKEND_CAPABILITY_UNAVAILABLE`;
  - a finished download's limit is refused;
  - the idempotency fingerprint is unchanged when no limit is given.
- **aria2:** both capabilities stay `unverified`, and a start with a limit is refused. There is no fixture evidence
  for aria2 limits.
- **CLI:**
  - `dm speed-limit`;
  - `dm speed-limit-set <N|off>`, with suffixes k/m/g meaning ×1000;
  - `dm job-speed-limit <JOB-ID> <N|off>`;
  - `dm add … --speed-limit N`.
- **Desktop:**
  - Options → overall speed limit;
  - download details → this download's limit;
  - KB/s, MB/s and GB/s in decimal units, with Bangla digits accepted;
  - unavailable reasons when the engine does not support limits;
  - English and Bangla, with labelled controls and status regions.

## 2. Queue schedules (CORE-08): what works

- **Domain (`dm-domain::schedule`, pure, `jiff`):**
  - one optional schedule per queue: enabled, IANA zone, 1–14 weekly windows and `skip | catch_up`;
  - overnight windows belong to their start day;
  - overlapping windows are merged;
  - DST: a boundary in a gap moves to the first instant after it; in a fold, a start takes the earlier occurrence and
    an end the later;
  - the evaluation takes an instant and returns `in_window` and `next_change`.
- **Service:**
  - **The blocker:** `schedule_window` is set or cleared at the start of every poll (at most 60 s apart) and on
    wake, before admission. It never touches `desired_state`: a window never resumes a manual pause and never starts
    a stopped queue, and the global and queue limits still apply.
  - **A window closing on running work:** a transfer with a durable checkpoint is paused like a queue stop. One
    without a checkpoint is allowed to finish (correction: resumability is judged from the durable checkpoint) and
    is re-checked at every evaluation.
  - **Catch-up:**
    - one window, for the most recent window missed within 24 h;
    - it lasts as long as the missed window, at most 6 h;
    - it is stored durably with absolute instants before it applies, so a restart neither repeats nor extends it;
    - an edit or removal clears it.
  - **Saving:** an unknown zone is refused before saving. A refused save changes nothing.
  - **Restart:** schedules are restored before recovery, so a restart outside a window starts nothing.
  - **Moves:** a moved job, and the jobs of a deleted queue, take the destination queue's stop state and schedule.
    Applying the destination's stop state on queue deletion is a fix found by the new test.
- **Contract:**
  - `queues.v1.set_schedule { queue_id, schedule | null }`;
  - `QueueInfo.schedule` and `schedule_state { in_window, next_change_at, catch_up_until }`, with UTC RFC 3339
    instants.
- **CLI:**
  - `dm queue-schedule <QUEUE-ID> <ZONE> <DAYS@HH:MM-HH:MM>... [skip|catch-up] [disabled]`;
  - `dm queue-schedule <QUEUE-ID> off`;
  - `dm queues` shows the schedule and its current state.
- **Desktop:** Manage queues → Schedule for each queue:
  - an enable checkbox;
  - the zone, preselected from the system;
  - windows with day checkboxes and From/Until times, plus an overnight note;
  - the missed-window policy, with an explanation;
  - the service's state, with the next change shown in the schedule's zone;
  - the fixed sentence that nothing downloads while the computer is off or asleep, and the policy sentence;
  - validation before anything is sent;
  - English and Bangla.

## 9. Defects found and fixed (each with a regression test that failed before the fix)

1. **A resumed job ignored its schedule** (`resume()`). With free slots, `resume` restarted a paused job's
   transfer at once, even in a closed window. Cause: its "can start now" check considered global and queue slots
   and the queue's running state, but not `schedule_window`. Fix: queue holds (`schedule_window`,
   `queue_stopped`) keep a resumed job waiting for admission. Test: `the_outside_window_exemption_is_reported_only_while_the_current_transfer_runs`.
2. **A retry dropped the queue's holds.**
   - When a retry was scheduled, the job's blockers were replaced wholesale. When it fell due, they were cleared.
   - So a retry could start outside its window. A queue stop that arrived during the cool-down was lost the same way
     (code reading; the test covers the schedule case).
   - Fix: the retry keeps `schedule_window` and `queue_stopped` (`is_queue_policy`); only its own cool-down blocker
     comes and goes.
   - Test: `an_exempt_transfer_is_held_at_its_first_checkpoint_and_a_failed_one_is_not_retried_outside_the_window`.
3. **A running transfer moved into a closed window kept running.**
   - Moving a job (`set_job_queue`), or deleting its queue, set `schedule_window` but did not pause the transfer.
     The window logic then skipped it as already held.
   - Fix: moves and deletions use the same per-job step as a window edge (`schedule_job`). A transfer with a
     checkpoint is paused and held; one without becomes exempt.
   - Queue deletion now also pauses running jobs moved into a stopped queue, as `set_job_queue` already did
     (code reading; no test of its own).
   - Test: `moving_a_running_checkpointed_transfer_into_a_closed_window_holds_it`.
4. **An exempt transfer was re-checked only every 60 s.** A checkpoint, or the end of the attempt, could go
   unnoticed for up to a minute. Fix: a queue with an exempt transfer is evaluated at every poll. The second test
   above covers it (the hold follows the first checkpoint at the next poll).
5. **Misleading `queue_limit`:** §8. Test: `queue_limit_is_shown_only_when_a_full_limit_is_the_reason`.
6. **Two schedule saves from one double click** (found by the packaged GUI run, G7).
   - Manage queues guarded its in-flight request with React state. Two clicks in one frame both saw "not busy".
   - Fix: a ref guard. Vitest `two clicks on Save schedule in the same frame send one request` was
     mutation-checked: with the old guard it fails.
7. **The per-download speed control was weaker than the overall one.**
   - It had the same state-based double-submit guard.
   - Its component was reused across selections, so a late answer for one download could show on another.
   - Fix: a ref guard, and the control is keyed by job. Vitest `never shows a late answer for one download on
     another, and saves once on a double click` was mutation-checked: without the key it fails.
8. **Bangla times showed a Latin "PM"** (`bn-BD` defaults to a 12-hour clock). The format is now 24-hour, like the
   HH:MM the windows are entered in.

## 10. Engine and scheduling review under adverse conditions

- **The budgets share one output.**
  - Each part is charged to the job bucket, then the global bucket, before it is written.
  - Every segment of a download shares that download's bucket.
  - Parts are no larger than the smaller bucket's burst (S01–S03, S06).
- **Cancellation and live changes:**
  - A dropped wait cancels it. At most one part's tokens can be spent without being written, which errs toward
    slower.
  - A rate change wakes waiters, which recompute with the new rate (S04).
- **No lock is held while waiting for tokens.**
  - The bucket's mutex is held only to refill and debit.
  - The segment's shared-progress mutex is scoped to an update after each write.
  - The coordinator and the store are never involved: waits happen in the engine's transfer tasks.
  - This was confirmed by reading `rate.rs`, `segmented.rs` and `transfer.rs`.
- **The unlimited path** reads one atomic (`part_limit() == None`) and writes the chunk whole.
- **Rotation under throttling:** a turn ends after 1 MiB or 2 s when others wait. Its bytes are flushed first, and
  the remaining range is re-requested with full validation (S02, S03; ADR 0023 tests unchanged).
- **The read timeout covers every network wait and no token wait:**
  - `within()` wraps every request send (each redirect hop is its own send) and every body read;
  - S05: 1 KiB/s with a 1 s timeout completes;
  - **new S07:** a server that never sends headers fails with `NetworkOffline` after 1.03 s, and one that stalls
    mid-body after about 3 s, both under a 2 KB/s limit.
- **Scheduling:**
  - policy is evaluated before admission;
  - manual pause, stopped queues, cancellation, retry back-off and the concurrency limits keep precedence (the
    fixes in §9 1–3);
  - schedules and limits are restored before recovery;
  - catch-up is bounded and durable. **New test** `clock_changes_and_restarts_never_repeat_or_extend_a_catch_up`
    covers a clock set back, then forward inside the original bounds, then far past the end, with wakes and
    restarts: never repeated, never extended;
  - a moved or deleted queue's jobs take the destination's policy;
  - the exemption stays limited to the current transfer.
- **Observations, not defects:**
  - **The fixture's server-side count can differ from the client count by megabytes over 20 s.** A download uses up
    to four range connections, and loopback socket buffers sit between the two counts. That is why the GUI
    criterion is the client view, as in ADR 0024's method (§11).
  - **Two minor accounting edge cases, left as they are:**
    - a wait cancelled after the job bucket was charged may cost one part of global tokens it did not use;
    - a pause takes effect at the next part boundary.

- **The timings are bimodal and order-dependent, for both packages alike.**
  - The second package of a 512 MiB pair took 7.5–8.3 s instead of about 4.2 s, every time.
  - That is consistent with the operating system still writing back the previous 512 MiB file. It was not
    investigated further.
- **Separating effects:**
  - the medians differ by −4 % and +7 % in opposite directions, inside that spread, so **no unthrottled
    regression is visible**;
  - deliberate throttling is measured separately: the GUI acceptance (§11) and S01–S06.
- **A measurement-tool defect, found and fixed:**
  - One `new` `active_1` run reported 2,844.6 MiB of private memory, against a median of 208.
  - `measure.mjs` builds the owned process tree from `ParentProcessId`. Windows reuses process IDs, and long-lived
    processes keep a parent ID whose process exited long ago: `wininit.exe` and `csrss.exe` name a boot-time
    `smss.exe`.
  - One of the run's WebView2 processes had such a reused ID, so the tool adopted `wininit` → `services` → 82
    `svchost` and the rest of the system: 165 processes instead of 8.
  - Fix: a child counts only if it started after its parent (CIM `CreationDate`).
  - The affected run and its pair are kept in `perf-results/b31/excluded/` and replaced by a new pair (run 6), made
    with the fixed tool. Earlier B2 measurements could have been hit the same way; their raw files show the process
    count.

  - an installed (not extracted) package was not measured;
  - IDM comparisons were not run.
